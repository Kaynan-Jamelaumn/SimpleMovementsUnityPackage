# 01 — Overview and Setup

## Two-minute setup

1. **Tools ▸ SimpleMovements ▸ Project Setup ▸ Create Crafting Database (Resources)** — the project's rules, filled
   with the built-in content (`Resources/SimpleMovements/Settings/CraftingDatabase`). Without it the built-in content is
   used as it is.
2. **Tools ▸ SimpleMovements ▸ Crafting ▸ Create Design Templates** — a weapon or armour asset per design
   (`Items/Crafting/Templates`): the attacks, models and sounds a crafted dagger, axe or breastplate copies. Assign your
   own art to them later; crafted items keep copying them.
3. **Tools ▸ SimpleMovements ▸ Crafting ▸ Create Test Station** — a station with every tool in the open scene. Walk up
   to it and press Interact: every material is free there and the window shows the numbers.
4. For a real game: **Create Smithy / Smeltery / Tannery / Leatherworks / Woodshop / Tailor's / Armourer / Spinnery /
   Lapidary / Jeweller's / Complete Workshop**, **Create Ore Deposit**, and **Create Stock Items** (bars, wire and strip,
   ore, hides and cloth, wooden blanks and logs, rough gems, fuel, supplies, agents, quenchants, fasteners as item
   assets) to put in merchants, loot tables and starting inventories.

The station needs a collider (the menus add one) so the Player Interactor finds it. Ore deposits are mined with a
weapon whose tool type is **Pick Axe** (its Tool Damage breaks the rock), or a few loose stones gathered by hand.

**Multiplayer**: with Fish-Net in the project, the menus also give the station a NetworkObject and a `NetCraftStation`
(an existing station: *Make Networked* in its inspector). The server then runs it and every player sees the same
workshop and the same crafted items - see [08 — Multiplayer](08-Multiplayer.md).

Condition sources on the station (the same components alchemy uses: `AlchemySurroundings` reads the weather and the
clock) set the ambient temperature and humidity: wet weather slows drying, a cold smithy cools metal faster.

**Craft Station Visuals** (the menus add it) draws the fire, the glow, the sparks, the steam and the sounds at the
station's points (*Forge / Anvil / Quench / Furnace / Grinder / Vat Point*; metal burning throws its sparks in the fire,
or the furnace, where it burns) and says where each work place worked by hand in 3D stands: the anvil's, the forge's,
the wheel's and the tank's points, and *Whetstone / Carving / Ore Bench / Mould Point* (the top of the bench the stone,
the blank or the ore lies on; the level of the mould's sand). Under *Worked by hand* give each work place the station's
own model of it (the anvil on its stump, the wheel, the tank...): its mesh renderers are hidden while the hands work

own effects never are. Without a model set, what of the station's own stands right where the work place is built (its
middle inside the work place, no bigger than a few times it) is hidden instead, the log says once that the model is
better set, and the station's inspector warns of a point set without its model. A work place without a point is built
just clear of the station - of all its colliders, its children's too - between it and the player (the forge, the anvil
and the tank without one in the row their effects use), its furniture down to the floor under them. The menus fill all
of it.

## From ore to a weapon

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    D[Ore seam] -->|pick| L[Ore lumps<br/>each keeps its seam]
    L -->|crush · brush · pick out the waste · pan · sieve| C[Cleaned ore]
    C -->|charge the furnace<br/>charcoal · bellows · limestone| F[Bloom + slag]
    F -->|tap slag · pull bloom| B[Spongy bloom]
    B -->|hammer at welding heat| BAR[Consolidated bar]
    S[Stock bars / ingots] --> BAR
    BAR -->|crucible: melt, cover, stir, skim, pour| CAST[Casting]
    BAR -->|forge: heat, strike, reheat| SHAPE[Shaped blank]
    CAST --> SHAPE
    SHAPE -->|grind · file| BEV[Bevels, tang, eye]
    BEV -->|heat · quench| HARD[Hardened]
    HARD -->|oven| TEMP[Tempered]
    TEMP -->|stones · strop| EDGE[Sharpened]
    H[Raw hide] -->|flesh · lime · unhair · tan · stretch, stake & dry · dye · oil| LEA[Leather]
    LEA -->|cut a pattern| PART[Strap, wrap, liner…]
    W[Wood blank] -->|saw to length · carve · rasp| HAFT[Grip, haft]
    EDGE --> ASM[Assembly bench]
    PART --> ASM
    HAFT --> ASM
    ASM -->|fit · peen · wedge · rivet · punch · stitch · wrap · finish · inspect| ITEM[Weapon / tool / armour]
```

A short session at the test station, making a hatchet (its head cast in bronze, its haft of ash):

1. Put two **Bronze bars** on the station (**Put**: at a test station every stock material is free) and each in the
   crucible (Crucible ▸ Into the crucible), light the forge (Forge ▸ Add fuel) and work the bellows in a steady rhythm
   until the melt runs bright; **Cover it** with borax once it has melted, **Stir**, **Skim**. Blow it no hotter than it
   needs to run: a melt held far too hot takes up gas (a porous casting).
2. Choose the mould **Hatchet: Hatchet head** - the pattern mould of the hatchet's own head, its 26 mm eye in it (what
   is cast there is meant for that part). It takes 616 g of bronze: two 315 g bars fill it, a few grams going to the
   dross (the stock *Axe head* mould takes some 1050 g, four bars, and is not the hatchet's shape). With too little metal
   a red tick on the FILL bar shows where it runs out. Press on the crucible and lift the pointer to tip it, carrying it
   sideways so the stream lands in the pouring cup: a steady, unbroken stream in the FLOW bar's green band fills the
   mould soundly; tip back as soon as it is full. Quench the casting to cool it (Quench ▸ Plunge, Lift out: bronze does
   not harden, it is only dipped to cool).
3. Put an **Ash blank** on the station: a stave 2.2 m long and 40 mm square. In **Carve**, choose what it is for
   (*Hatchet: Haft* - the haft's shape, 380-480 mm long and about 32 mm square, is dashed over it), **Saw to length** at
   about 430 mm (click to mark the cut, press on the mark and saw up and down; then pick the piece you keep) and shave
   it to the dashed shape (*Shave the width*, *Shave the thickness*: a bite past 1.4 mm splits it). The end that goes
   into the head is framed in gold, *EYE END 26 MM*: rasp it down (Grind ▸ *Shape: Rasp* ▸ Tang, holding on that end)
   until it is no more than 3 mm thicker than the eye - 26 to 29 mm, a little tight is a good drive. At the assembly
   bench, **Start** a *Hatchet* and fit the head, then the haft (**Place**, the piece, **Fit it**; a haft still too
   thick is refused with how much: *"It will not go on: the haft is 6 mm too thick. Rasp it down."*). The guide now
   reads *"Head on the haft: drive the wedge home"*: hold under the joints to raise the hammer, let go in the green band
   of the blow meter, until the gauge beside it reads *home* (0.6-1.3; past its band the haft splits).
4. Grind the bevels on the fitted head (pick it under the hatchet in the piece list, or **Go** on the guide's *Grind and
   sharpen its edge: grind the bevels first*; Grind ▸ *Grind: Coarse wheel* ▸ Left/Right bevel at 15°: hold on the edge
   and move along it, the two bevels in turn, until the strip is green; press lighter as it gets hot - a fitted head
   cannot go in the tank), then sharpen it on the medium stone: each face with strokes into the edge until its bar
   fills, then light trailing strokes to take the burr off.
5. **Finish** it: at a test station it comes out tried, so its quality, edge, heat treatment and assembly show on the
   right at once, with every note on it (at a normal station **Try it** shows them). **Take** it. (Taken untried from a
   normal station, it can be tried later from the bag: *Try that* at a bench where hatchets are made.)

A steel blade goes through the forge and anvil instead of the mould, and through the quench tank and the oven before
the stones: see [02 — Simulation Rules](02-Simulation-Rules.md).

## From the log, the fleece and the rough stone

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    LOG[Green log in its bark] -->|debark at the shaving horse| DL[Debarked log]
    DL -->|split with wedges and maul| HALF[Halves]
    HALF -->|rive · saw| BL[Billets, boards]
    BL -->|stack: stickers, sealed ends, shade| SEA[Seasoned wood]
    SEA -->|sand coarse, medium, fine · seal| FIN[Finished part]
    FL[Raw fleece] -->|pick · scour · dry · tease| OF[Open, clean fibre]
    OF -->|card - or comb, for long staple| PREP[Rolags / combed top]
    PREP -->|spin at the wheel| YARN[Bobbins of singles]
    YARN -->|ply · wind a skein and count it| SK[Measured skein]
    SK -->|reel| TH[Thread items]
    YARN -->|wind bobbins together · tension the warp · beat each row in at the loom| CL[Woven cloth]
    CL --> SEW[Measure the wearer · lay out · cut · sew]
    TH --> SEW
    R[Rough stone, crusted] -->|scrub off the matrix: brush or pick| CR[Clean rough]
    CR -->|loupe · hardness · refractometer · balance · dichroscope| ID[Identified]
    ID -->|search with the loupe| EV[Evaluated]
    EV -->|orient the cut · split along the cleavage, nick and snap, or saw| PRE[Sawn preform]
    PRE -->|grind to the outline on the wheel| GP[Ground preform]
    GP -->|facet each tier on the timing bar · polish every region| GEM[Cut stone]
    W[Wire, bezel strip] -->|size the finger · form round the mandrel| PARTS[Band, bezel, prongs]
    GEM -->|cut the seat to the girdle| PARTS
    PARTS -->|solder · set the stone · buff · inspect| JEWEL[Ring, pendant, necklace, circlet, bracelet, brooch, charm]
```

Every step leaves its mark on the next: bark left on wet wood rots it and slows its drying, a gouge costs width, a
split off the heart gives an uneven pair, wood seasons only as dry as the air allows; felted or greasy fibre spins
lumpy (drafted fine, lumpier still), combed fibre spins a strong worsted, a balanced ply hangs straight, a miscounted
skein reels fewer threads, a lumpy yarn is harder to beat in evenly and a skipped pick is a flaw the tailor must cut round; crust left on a rough
stone blurs its readings and hides its flaws, a misread refractive index gives the wrong cutting chart, a split off its
measure steps the face, a preform never ground comes out lopsided, uneven facets cost the cut and no polish mends them,
a table laid on a cleavage flakes, a seat cut wide never holds its stone.

### The timing bar

Sawing, splitting, faceting and the loom's beat share one mechanic: an indicator runs along a bar and back, the bar
carries one or more targets with nested zones (**Perfect**, **Excellent**, **Good**, **Poor**; beyond them a **Miss**),
and you strike when the indicator is on a target. The score is the real distance from the target's ideal point - 100%
on the white mark, falling continuously through each zone's edge score - not a fixed value per zone; it shows as a
percentage with its grade. Below the damage threshold the work is harmed (a chip, a crack, a skipped pick, lost weight).
Harder or more brittle stones and lumpier yarn narrow the zones and speed the indicator; practice widens them; a tired,
trembling hand moves the hit. Some bars carry two targets: a narrow one that gives the most, and a wide, safe one that
gives less. Every zone width, the edge scores, the damage threshold, the speed and how much difficulty and practice
change them are set in **Crafting Database → Simulation → Timing bars**.

## The window

* **Left** — your materials the station takes (with **Put**, or **All** for up to twenty at once — it reads **+20** when you
  have more — each still a piece of its own), and the pieces on the station: click one to work it; **Take** puts it in
  your inventory (a worked-piece item, or the finished item). Hot metal and hot ore must cool first, and a piece inside a
  working place — the furnace (ore smelts there; a casting put back in comes out with the tongs), the quench tank, the oven, the ashes, a vat, the steam box — is
  taken out first (a blade lifted out of the tank is a quench your journal learns from). The window says so at once and
  leaves the work in hand on the piece as it is; so it does while work of several presses is under way on it
  (*"Finish cutting the tier of facets first."*). The parts fitted to an assembly are listed under it, indented: the areas that
  finish a part in place (**Grind**, **Sharpen**) can pick one — a blade in its hilt, a head on its haft — the others
  open on the assembly. The lists are updated in place, so a click is never lost while something heats or cools.
* **Messages** — two lines under the work surface say what the station answered (a refusal says why; work your hands did
  faster than real hands can is granted only in part, and says so: *"Too fast: 4 of those 6 strokes counted."*), what
  was learnt and when the arm gets worse; each stays long enough to read (6 s, longer for a long one) unless a newer one
  replaces it, and every one also goes into *What happened* (refusals dimmed), with what the station shows happening, in
  sentences (*"Something pings as it cools."*, *"Off the wheel: long streaks with a few forks."*, *"The batch is all
  spun."*) - never an id or a name the tests have yet to tell; a piece made and a shovel of fuel are not logged (the
  areas say them in their answers). A piece taken into the bag says where it came from the same way (*"Rived from an
  ash blank."*, *"A batch of raw wool."*). In a network session the answer comes a
  round trip later, once your copy of the workshop shows what the work did (an answer that never comes is
  *"No answer from the station."* after 10 s). Under them, the area's hint (how it is worked) and, at its end, the arm.
* **The pointer** means the same in every area: **right-click puts down or aborts what the hands are doing** (it never
  commits, never destroys, never acts as a left click); **the wheel turns or adjusts** what is in hand, a notch at a
  time; everything else is a left click or a button. Only the left button works the surface (the middle one does
  nothing, and a second button never ends or fires the first one's stroke). Strokes are measured by the way the hand
  went, not by the frame rate, from the first pixel (there is no drag threshold). Every area's hint ends with what
  right-click does there: *puts down* where the hands just let go, and in a few its own act - the anvil and the assembly
  bench *lower the hammer*, the crucible *tips back*, the wheel *lifts it off*, the stone *drops a stroke*, the cutting
  table *lifts the knife*, the carving bench *lifts the tool*.
* **Colours** mean the same in every area: on a meter, green is the safe or ideal band, amber barely or not yet, red
  harm, blue too cold or too slow to take, grey does nothing yet; the white tick is the value; gold is what the hands
  picked or aim at; the design's target is dashed green; metal glows as its heat does. Every meter and gauge is named on
  the surface, in one lettering (capitals, a reading in its units: *MM*, *°C*, *×3*).
* **Irreversible actions** ask first: at a test station **Scrap** and **Clear all** take a second click within
  2.5 s (*"Click Scrap again to throw this piece away."*); *Clear all* is there only where the station's rules run
  (offline, or the host). At any station, **Scrap** gives up an item nothing has gone into yet (a design started by
  mistake: nothing fitted, added or worked), asked twice too (*"Click Scrap again to give up this item: nothing has gone
  into it yet."*); the measurement it took waits again for the next item started.
* **Size** — at 1920 × 1080 the work surface is shown pixel for pixel; on a smaller or wider screen the whole window
  scales down to fit (again whenever the window is resized).
* **Visits** — another station (or another player) starts with fresh work areas, so nothing held in the window moves
  between stations; closing the window and opening it again at the same station keeps them and opens the area you
  left. Work of several presses still under way when the window closes (a tier of facets half cut, a saw half through,
  the rivets being made...) is said to be left unfinished (*"You stopped cutting the tier of facets before it was
  done."*).
* **Next** — in the right column, under what the piece looks like, a line says what comes next for it, read from its state (*"Clean the
  crust off it (it blurs the tests and hides flaws)"*, *"Card it - or comb it, for a smooth worsted"*, *"Crown seam:
  punch and stitch it (12 of 20 stitches)"*), with **Go** to open that work area at that place - named as its button reads
  (*Tailor → Lay out*, *Woodshop → Steam & bend*, *Finish leather → The bench*). The line keeps its place: with no step it
  says so, dimmed (*"Next: nothing in particular."*). It only says what the state shows: optional steps
  are named as such, and nothing is suggested where the piece's path is the player's own choice (a bar of steel; wire
  and strip, or a part meant for a jewellery design, are told to go round the mandrel). **Go** shows only when this
  station has that area *and* that place; otherwise the line says so (*"Lapidary → Facet: not at this station"*), or
  the step itself says where to take the piece (*"Take it to a tailor's table to lay out and cut its patterns"*), and
  a step done in the left column (*"Take it: it is done"*) has no Go. It follows the piece where it is and what the
  station has: clean ore goes to the furnace (an unroasted sulfide to be roasted there first; the step says whether it
  gives a bloom to pull or metal to run off), calamine to the crucible with molten copper under a cover (a furnace would
  only boil its zinc away), ore in the furnace is smelting, ore in the crucible gives its metal to the melt; a copper
  matte or lead carrying silver goes back into the furnace, to be roasted or cupelled; with nothing picked, it says what
  the furnace holds once its charge is spent (metal to run off into a mould, a bead to lift out with the tongs, a bloom
  to pull); the bloom pulled is closed up at a forge before it is anything (*"Close the bloom up: strike it with flat
  blows while it is at welding heat, until it is a sound bar (struck cold, it crumbles)"*: the **Anvil**, or the
  **Forge** for another welding heat; from a smeltery, which has no forge, *"Take the bloom to a forge..."*), and the
  step goes once it is a sound bar; a bar whose forge weld is still open is told to set it (*"Set the weld: strike the
  seam shut with the flat of the hammer while it is at welding heat"*: the **Anvil** while it is that hot, else back to
  the **Forge**). A log is debarked (with a shaving horse) and split, or rived when it is
  too narrow to split; green split stock is split on into billets before it seasons (*"Split the half again with the
  wedges, into quarters - green wood is split before it seasons"*), and stock meant for a part is split or rived while
  each half would still hold the part (*"Rive it with the froe: the short haft wants 27 x 27 mm, it is 58 x 58 mm"*);
  green wood is seasoned in the stack, a part meant for a curve (a scythe's snath) steamed and bent to it at the
  Woodshop's Bend; a blank meant for a wooden part whose length is off by as much as the finished item would note (a
  stock blank: the 2.2 m ash stave meant for a haft of 380-480 mm) is sawn to length first, before it is bent (*"Saw it
  to length (the haft wants 380-480 mm, it is 2200 mm)"*: **Carve** ▸ *Saw to length*, or the Woodshop's Saw where there
  is only a sawhorse), and one too short is told to use a longer blank; seasoned, a part still wider or thicker than its
  shape (by the evaluator's own measure) is carved to it (*"Carve it to its shape: shave its width down to the dashed
  outline"*: **Carve** ▸ *Shave the width*), a grip bored for its tang (**Carve** ▸ *Bore the slot*), and then sanded
  through the grits and sealed, if the woodworker likes (*"Sand the haft through the grits, coarse to fine, and seal it
  (optional: ...)"*: **Woodshop** ▸ *Finish*); a hide goes along the tannery's path at the place that does each step (the beam, the vats,
  the frame: out of a vat that is not doing what it needs, *LOOSE* past 70% in the lime, *CORE* past 75% in the tan,
  staked while it is damp as it dries), a dried one that dried stiff is worked supple (bark-tanned, oiled - staked dry,
  its grain cracks; of another tannage, staked) and cut at a leather bench, a cut part
  goes into an item, cloth goes to a tailor's table (or, where there is none, to a leather bench's Cut); a bobbin too
  short for the cloth part an item here still wants is told to have another wound on at the loom (*Wind on*). For an
  item: its parts in order (a wooden part with the length it is judged by, *"Fit its parts: haft first (wood, 380-480 mm
  long)"*; a wooden part fitted at a length the item would note is taken off again first: *"Staff: too long (2200 mm,
  wants 1700-2000 mm) - take it off, saw it to length and fit it again"* - taken off, it is still meant for that part,
  so its own line sends it to the saw; a set of buckles or rivets short of what the design takes asks for another set; the thread
  with the parts where the bench sews with the item's own thread), then its joints, each by its work and how far it has
  come (*"Head on the haft: drive the wedge home"*, *"Boss riveted on: set the rivets (2 of 4)"*) - jewellery is
  soldered before the stone goes in (the torch's heat cracks stones) and the setting's seat is cut to the stone first; a
  cord no seam takes is named at the end; a part left off that is optional (a grip wrap) holds nothing up. Before
  *Finish it*, a blade or a head that is not yet keen is sent to **Grind** (no bevels yet) or **Sharpen**, where the
  station has the wheel or the whetstone - **Go** then picks the fitted part itself (of three throwing knives, the one
  still dull); once finished, *"Try it to see what it is"*.
* **Tabs** — a station with many work areas (more than eight, of more than one craft) groups them by craft: a first
  row picks the craft (Smithing, Leatherwork, Woodwork, Tailoring, Jewellery, and Assemble), the second shows its
  areas.
* **Practice** — the subtitle names the station's instruments (*"Instruments: calipers, balance, loupe, magnet, test
  file, refractometer, set of hardness points, dichroscope"*; at a test station *every tool and instrument*) and shows
  the player's rank in the craft being worked (*"Tailoring: Journeyman tailor, 42% to expert"*); a button for work done
  by routine shows the rank it needs, greyed, until the player has it (chapter 04, *Practice*).
* **Middle** — the work areas this station has, each a surface drawn from the real state:

| Area | How it is worked |
|---|---|

### By hand, in 3D

Eight of the areas can also be worked with your own hands at the real work place: press the area's **by hand** button
(or the *By Hand* key, **H**). The window steps aside, the view moves to the anvil (the forge, the tank...), seen from
the side of the station you stand on - the other side when a wall stands close on yours and that side has the room;
something standing in its way draws the view nearer (the station's own anvil, wheel or tank steps aside for the work
place's - the model set for it under *Worked by hand*, else what of the station's own stands right where the work place
is built - and so does your own body), and the mouse becomes the hand: there is no cursor - what it carries moves the
way it moves on the screen. The piece is
there as it really is — its shape segment by segment, each segment glowing at its own heat, the bevels you ground (on
both edges of a double-edged blade), the bends you hammered, the eye you drifted, the fuller's groove, a dish, a cut
half made, the cracks that show, its scale, polish and temper colours, a glassy skin of molten flux, a burnt stretch
dull and dark — and changes as you work it. *Put Down* (**right-click** or **Q**) puts the
tool down and brings the window back; **Escape** goes back to the game. The work goes on as it is either way.
The work place starts with what the window chose (the tool and face, the stone, the side and angle, the entry into the
tank, the brush, the cut, the stretch of the piece framed for the fire or the stone - even only its first segment), and
what you choose by hand comes back to the window's controls when you put the tool down. The knife does not saw by hand:
a window set to *Saw to length* starts it shaving the width, and keeps its saw unless you choose another cut there. The
tool is not taken up while a stroke is under way on the drawing or the area has work of several presses going
(*"Finish what your hands are doing first."*), and a button already held when it is taken up only counts once it has
been let go.

**The keys are input actions.** Everything worked by hand here and at the alchemy station (the spoon, the bellows, the
filter, the mortar) reads the actions of a **Station** map (`StationControls`): `StationWork`, `StationPutDown`,
`StationNextTool`, `StationQuick`, `StationTurnOver`, `StationFire`, `StationFaceEdge`, `StationFeedFire`,
`StationVentClose` / `StationVentOpen` (*Less* / *More*), `StationConfirm`, `StationByHand`,
`StationChoice1`–`StationChoice10`, `StationPrevChoice` / `StationNextChoice`, and the values `StationPoint` (where the
hand is), `StationScroll` and `StationMoveHand` (a gamepad stick moves the hand). Add the map to your input actions
asset to choose the keys there; any action missing is created at start with the default keys in the table below. They
are rebindable in *Settings ▸ Key Bindings* (their own section), saved with the other keys, and every hint on screen
shows the key actually bound — for a gamepad, its buttons. While the hands are at work the player's gameplay keys are
off, so R, F, Q, Tab or Space never also cast, dash or open the inventory.

### The window or the hands: which to use

Both are the same workshop and the same requests, but they are not the same work:

| | The window | By hand, in 3D |
|---|---|---|
| Pace | careful: a hand stroke in the window does **60 %** of the work of one at the real wheel, file or knife for a novice, up to **90 %** for a master (the player's practice in that craft, chapter 04) | the full pace (`handWorkPace`) |
| What you see | diagrams: the magnified section, the target dashed over it, strips along the length, exact spans to choose | the real piece, its glow, the sparks and the sound: you judge by eye and ear |
| Good for | the fine finishing: the last passes of a bevel, a precise edge, a delicate weld, learning what each tool does | the rough work: hogging off steel, drawing out, a long grind, carving the bulk of a grip |

A novice is safer in the window (the target shows when to stop); an experienced smith roughs out by hand and finishes
in the window, or does it all by hand. *Simulation Settings ▸ Hand-work pace / Window pace (novice, master)* tune it.

### A tired arm

Every stroke tires the arm (a full hammer blow most, a light stroke little) - a stroke by the time the hand works at
it, a movement every tenth of a second, not by how often the screen samples it, so a fast display tires it no faster;
it recovers slowly while you keep working and properly once you stop for a couple of seconds (about half the tiredness
goes in twenty seconds). A bar at the end of the hint line under the work surface (and *Arms* in 3D) shows it - green
while fresh, gold once it tires (from 35%), orange when it is heavy (60%), red and trembling when it is spent (85%), the
steps its words change at - and when it gets worse the message line says how it feels, in the words of the work in hand
(the blows of the hammer, the maul, the mallet; the hand's own work everywhere else: *"Your arm is tiring: the hand
works lighter and less true."*). The arm is the player's for the session, at every station:
closing the window does not rest it at once — it rests while the window is closed as it would at the bench. A tired
arm is:

* **weaker** — the hammer cannot be lifted as high (the blows land lighter; in 3D the hammer rises less), the bellows
  puff less, the wheel and the stone are pressed less hard, a stitch is pulled less tight;
* **less precise** — blows wander off their mark and tilt, strokes wobble;
* **slipping** — past about half tired, now and then the hammer glances off (*"The hammer slipped: a glancing
  blow"*: off its mark, tilted, weak — in 3D it twists aside) or the knife skids and digs in (a split in wood). The
  more tired, the more often; practice makes it rarer; a rushed blow slips more.

The chance is seeded, so the same hands doing the same thing slip the same way (replays and tests stay exact). The
station also keeps its own model of each player's arm (it tires with the blows it lets through and rests in real time)
and holds every blow's force to what that arm has left - offline, on the host and for a network client alike (a test
station holds nobody's).

### While working

While a station's window (or its 3D work) is open the player **stands at it**: the move keys, jumping, dashing and rolling do nothing (`MovementStateMachine.ReadDeviceInput` reads a neutral input while `InputBindingStore.GameplayInputBlocked`), in a network session too. A station's window (and the 3D work) owns the screen and the keys: the hotbar, the abilities bar and the rest of the
HUD are hidden, **Tab** does not open the inventory and the number keys do not switch the hotbar — they choose tools.
Closing the window (or **Escape**) gives them back. A panel that should behave like this implements `IExclusivePanel`.

On a normal station everything is done with the hands: there are no "do it" buttons (they are only at a test station,
for trying things quickly) — fluxing, folding, welding, rinsing, sieving, covering, stirring, skimming, claying,
burying in the ashes, filling and dosing the vats are each a small action as above.

* **Right** — what the selected piece looks, sounds and feels like and what the instruments say (once a metal or an
  ore is assayed, the assay's readout under it, an element a line; the assay kit's row with its **Assay** button only
  at a station that has the kit); what comes next; the fires, the furnace, the crucible, the tank, the oven and the
  vats; what just happened. At a test station also the numbers and the time buttons.
* **Journal** (top right) — your mastery of each craft, metals, ores, heat colours, quenches, what you have made and
  how, insights, recent work.

The player's hands tremble a little (more as they tire, less with practice): the same input never gives exactly the
same blow, but the tremble is seeded, so replays stay exact.
